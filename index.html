How we build

We own the parts that are hard to own.

Renting the difficult pieces is faster for a quarter and fatal for a decade. The margin, the latency and the failure modes all live in the parts you cannot see into — so we run them.

  • 01 — Infrastructure

    Our own servers carry the calls

    Signaling, presence and the TURN relay are ours, on hardware we pay for. A per-minute product cannot survive a per-minute vendor, and you cannot debug a dropped call through somebody else's API.

  • 02 — Correctness

    Money moves inside a transaction

    Balances are rows in Postgres, changed under a lock, with payments keyed so the same rupee cannot land twice. Where money is involved, eventually consistent is another way of saying sometimes wrong.

  • 03 — Compatibility

    We cannot update a phone we already shipped

    Every deploy is checked against a recorded contract of what installed builds depend on. If a change would break a version already on someone's phone, the deploy stops before it reaches them.

  • 04 — Evidence

    Measured, then claimed

    The number at the top of this page came off a load test, not a slide. We would rather publish a figure we can reproduce than a superlative we cannot.

Where this goes

Consumer products fund the hard problems.

Real-time media that survives a congested cell tower. Models small enough to run on a phone that cost eight thousand rupees. Payments, identity and trust for people whose first computer was the one in their pocket.

None of these are solved, and none of them get solved by people who have never had to live with the constraint. We have. That is the whole thesis.
